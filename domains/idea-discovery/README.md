# Idea discovery

Turn the user's real skills into product ideas worth testing, and pick one with a
cheap validation path.

## Use when

The user asks what to build: a SaaS or product idea, a side project, a way to
monetise a skill, or a choice between two ideas. Not for market research on an
idea they have already committed to building.

## Inputs

From the profile where possible, otherwise ask in one message:

- what they can build unaided today, and what they have shipped
- domain knowledge from a job, hobby or community (the unfair advantage)
- hours per week and runway available
- money they can spend before revenue
- who they can reach without paying for ads (audience, network, former
  colleagues, a community)
- outcome sought: income, learning, portfolio, or a company

## Workflow

1. **Inventory the advantage.** List 3–6 assets: skills, domains, access,
   audience, credentials. No idea may be proposed before this list exists.
2. **Mine lived pain.** For each asset, list the recurring, budgeted, annoying
   problems the user has personally seen — from their job, not from a trends
   article.
3. **Generate 6–8 candidates.** Each one line: who pays, for what, why this user.
   Deliberately include two "boring" candidates; they often score best.
4. **Score and cut.** Apply the rubric in `references/methodology.md`. Keep the
   top three, then one recommendation.
5. **Design the cheapest test.** For the recommendation: the smallest artifact
   that produces evidence (interview script, landing page, concierge service),
   its cost, and the date it runs.
6. **Define the first slice.** Two weeks of build, scoped to one workflow, with
   the demo the user can show a potential customer.

## Deliverable

1. A table of all candidates with rubric scores and a one-line verdict each.
2. A one-pager for the recommendation: customer, problem, wedge, first product,
   pricing hypothesis, route to the first ten users, validation test, kill
   criteria.
3. The two-week plan: what to build, what to say, who to talk to, what evidence
   makes it continue or stop.

## Quality bar

- The recommendation must use an asset the user already has; otherwise it is a
  hobby, not a fit.
- Every idea names a payer and a reachable channel. "SEO" and "viral" are not
  channels for a first customer.
- The validation test costs under the user's stated budget and finishes inside
  the stated runway.
- Kill criteria are numeric and written before the test runs.

## Out of scope

Legal formation, cap tables, fundraising decks and financial modelling beyond a
pricing hypothesis. Say so if asked.
