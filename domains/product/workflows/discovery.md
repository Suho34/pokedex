# Product discovery

Generate product ideas worth testing from the user's real advantages, then pick
one and design its cheapest validation.

## Use when

The user has no idea yet: "what should I build", "give me a SaaS idea", "what
side project fits me", "how do I make money from my skills". If they already have
an idea and want a verdict, use `product.evaluation`.

## Inputs

Skills with evidence, hours per week, budget, reachable audience, desired outcome
— from the profile, otherwise asked in one message.

## Steps

1. **Inventory the advantage.** List 3–6 assets: skills, domains, access,
   audience, credentials. No idea may be proposed before this list exists.
2. **Mine lived pain.** For each asset, list recurring, budgeted, annoying
   problems the user has personally seen at work — not trends from articles.
3. **Generate 6–8 candidates.** One line each: who pays, for what, why this user.
   Include two deliberately boring candidates; they often score best.
4. **Score and cut.** Apply the rubric in `references/methodology.md`. Keep the
   top three, then make one recommendation.
5. **Design the cheapest test.** The smallest artifact that produces evidence
   (interview script, priced landing page, concierge delivery), its cost, and the
   date it runs.
6. **Define the first slice.** Two weeks of build, one workflow, and the demo the
   user can show a potential customer.

## Deliverable

1. A table of all candidates with rubric scores and a one-line verdict each.
2. A one-pager for the recommendation: customer, problem, wedge, first product,
   pricing hypothesis, route to the first ten users, validation test, kill
   criteria.
3. A two-week plan: what to build, what to say, who to talk to, and what evidence
   means continue or stop.

## Feasibility

Judge it here, from the user's own skills and hours. If the idea's core needs a
skill the profile does not show, either price the learning into the timeline or
cut the idea. Never assume unlimited time.

## Quality bar

- The recommendation uses an asset the user already has; otherwise it is a hobby.
- Every idea names a payer and a reachable channel.
- The validation test costs less than the stated budget and fits the runway.
- Kill criteria are numeric and written before the test runs.

## When combined with another workflow

If the user also wants the idea to serve an internship or portfolio, add
`career.job-preparation` as a supporting workflow and make the two-week slice
demonstrable in an interview: one shipped workflow, a README, and one measured
result. Do not add the career module unless the request names that second
deliverable.
