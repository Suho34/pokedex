# Career planning

Turn a vague ambition into a dated plan with proof artifacts, built from what the
user has actually done.

## Use when

The user asks where to go next: a target role, a promotion, a pivot, skill gaps,
or "what should I learn". Not for rewriting documents (that is resume work) and
not for interview rehearsal (that is job preparation).

## Inputs

From the profile where possible, otherwise ask in one message:

- current role, employer type, and dates
- target role or outcome, and when they want it
- what they can already prove with artifacts or work history
- constraints: hours per week, money, location, work authorisation, notice period
- risk appetite: stay and grow, or leave

## Workflow

1. **Audit evidence.** List the user's claims next to the artifact that proves
   each one. Claims without artifacts are marked as unproven.
2. **Define the target concretely.** One role title, one seniority level, one
   market (city or remote region), one date.
3. **Reverse-engineer requirements.** Use 8–10 real job posts for that target;
   extract the recurring requirements. See `references/methodology.md`.
4. **Build the gap table.** Requirement, current evidence, gap size, cheapest
   proof, effort in hours.
5. **Sequence the plan.** 90 days, 12 months, 24 months, with the artifact that
   closes each gap and the date it exists.
6. **Set the review cadence.** A monthly check with a decision point, and the
   conditions that mean "change the target".

## Deliverable

1. Target statement: role, level, market, date.
2. Gap table with effort estimates, sorted by requirement frequency.
3. Sequenced plan: quarter by quarter, each item naming its artifact and hours
   per week.
4. Two or three decision points with explicit criteria.
5. The first action for this week.

## Quality bar

- Every plan item produces an artifact someone else can inspect: a deployed
  project, a written design, a measured result, a talk, a contribution.
- The weekly time budget matches the user's stated hours; do not silently assume
  twenty hours.
- No item exists only to collect a certificate, unless the target market
  genuinely filters on it — say which postings require it.
- The plan survives a missed week: state what gets dropped first.

## Out of scope

Salary negotiation scripts (job preparation), resume rewrites (resume module),
and predictions about hiring markets beyond what the collected postings show.
